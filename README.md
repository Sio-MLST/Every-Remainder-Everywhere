**Every-Remainder-Everywhere**

We prove that for every prime $p$, every positive integer $w$ not divisible by $3$, every residue class $r \in \mathbb{Z}/p\mathbb{Z}$, and every threshold $B \in \mathbb{N}$, there exists an integer $n > B$ such that $n$ reaches $w$ under iterations of the Collatz map and $n \equiv r \pmod p$. In particular, the set of reaching predecessors of $w$ in any residue class modulo $p$ is infinite. The proof proceeds by analyzing an explicit two-branch affine dynamical system on the $3$-adic integers. We define a family of finite fibres recording the residues modulo $p$ attained by descendants of an adic seed; show that their cardinalities stabilize; and prove that an affine holonomy group acts transitively on the stabilized fibres via a non-trivial translation generated by a group commutator. The entire mathematical development is formalization-faithful, matching a complete verification in Lean 4 with Mathlib. Finally, we establish a no-go-theorem that single-prime modular obstructions can not rule out the Diophantine equations of hypothetical Collatz cycles.
